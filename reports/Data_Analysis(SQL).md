# Retail Sales Analysis — Business Findings Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The retail business generated **12.63M in sales** and **1.47M in profit** across **25,717 orders** and **17,411 customers**, resulting in an overall profit margin of approximately **11.60%**.

The business shows strong growth over the four-year period from 2012 to 2015. However, the analysis reveals several important profitability risks.

The most significant issue is **excessive discounting**. Profitability declines consistently as discounts increase, with discounts above 20% becoming unprofitable and discounts above 40% generating extremely severe losses.

Other major concerns include **679 loss-making products, 4,425 loss-making customers, and three loss-making regions**.

The analysis suggests that the company should focus not only on increasing sales but also on improving **profitability, pricing discipline, discount management, product selection, customer profitability, and regional performance**.

# 4. Discounting Is the Biggest Profitability Risk

The strongest finding from the analysis is the relationship between discounting and profitability.

| Discount Range | Sales | Profit | Profit Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 7.25M | 1.83M | **25.21%** |
| 1–10% | 1.70M | 279.82K | **16.48%** |
| 11–20% | 1.77M | 172.38K | **9.73%** |
| 21–30% | 366.34K | -20.56K | **-5.61%** |
| 31–40% | 735.08K | -177.55K | **-24.15%** |
| 40%+ | 812.39K | -615.83K | **-75.80%** |

### Finding

There is a clear profitability threshold.

- Discounts up to 20% remain profitable overall.
- Discounts between 21–30% begin generating losses.
- Discounts between 31–40% create significant losses.
- Discounts above 40% produce extremely severe losses.

The **40%+ discount group alone generated approximately 615.8K in losses**.

### Business implication

The company should establish **discount limits and approval rules** instead of allowing aggressive discounts without considering product margins.

# 5. High Discounts Hurt Every Major Category

At discounts above 40%, all three major categories become heavily unprofitable.

| Category | Profit | Profit Margin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Furniture | -221.02K | -79.61% |
| Office Supplies | -201.71K | -76.67% |

### Finding

The problem is not limited to one category.

Extreme discounting negatively affects:

- Furniture
- Office Supplies
- Technology

**Furniture experiences the largest absolute loss.**

### Business implication

Discount policies should be based on **product/category margin**, rather than applying the same discount strategy across all products.

### Finding

**4,425 customers are loss-making.**

These customers generated approximately:

This is a major warning because high customer revenue does not necessarily mean the customer is financially valuable.

### Business implication

The company should identify why these customers are unprofitable.

Potential causes include:

- Excessive discounts
- High shipping costs
- Low-margin products
- Frequent returns
- Unprofitable purchasing patterns

# 12. Shipping Cost Impact

Shipping performance by shipping mode shows:

| Shipping Mode | Shipping Cost % |
|---|---:|
| Standard Class | **8.16%** |
| Second Class | 12.28% |
| First Class | 16.85% |
| Same Day | **17.41%** |

### Finding

Standard Class has the lowest shipping-cost percentage, while Same Day has the highest.

### Business implication

Premium shipping should be used strategically, particularly for orders with:

- Low margins
- High discounts
- Low order values

Otherwise, shipping costs can further reduce already-thin margins.

# 14. Recommended Business Actions

## Priority 1 — Control Discounts

Implement a discount policy such as:

```text
0–10%     → Normal discount
11–20%    → Controlled discount
21–30%    → Management review
31–40%    → Strong approval required
40%+      → Avoid unless strategically justified
